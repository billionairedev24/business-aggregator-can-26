Three directions to choose from

Each direction takes Amazon’s marketplace patterns (search-led header, department strip, tiles then rails, a filter rail, a buy box, one-page checkout, a Seller Central-style back office) and none of its brand. All three fix what the October audit found:

  • One sans family for all UI text
  • Buttons, tabs, chips and segments that never look alike
  • A visible selected state on every choice
  • One action colour, one commerce colour, semantic status colours with a red destructive
  • One price format ($8.50 / 8,50 $) and real photos with a designed fallback
Direction A

Plainsight

Product on white, nothing in the way. A crisp, dense catalogue where photos, prices and delivery promises do the talking.

Plainsight is the closest to how a large marketplace actually works: a search bar you cannot miss, many items per screen, and a calm white canvas so merchants’ photos and prices carry the page. Photos sit in uniform grey wells, which makes amateur merchant photos look catalogued instead of messy. One family, IBM Plex Sans, holds up at 12 px meta sizes and lines numbers up in tables. Corners are almost square, so a button, a tab, a chip and a segmented control differ by shape alone, and anything selected turns solid ink. Blue is the only action colour; magenta is kept for deals and the cart count. It is the easiest direction to build and keep consistent across 125 routes. The cost is personality: it leans on the copy and the photos to feel local.

Amazon patterns it uses

  • Search-led header: logo, deliver-to, search with a department scope, account and orders, cart count
  • Grey department strip with deals at the right end
  • Delivery-cutoff promo strip, four category tiles with a deal-of-the-day tile, then rails
  • Left filter rail with counts; applied filters as removable chips; 5-up dense grid
  • Gallery with a thumbnail column, details, and a bordered buy box
  • One-page checkout as one stacked form
  • Seller Central-style action cards with a status edge; dense tables with a dark bulk bar

Direction B

Main Street

The neighbourhood marketplace. Warm and photo-led: you meet the baker, the barber and the kitchen before the SKU.

Main Street keeps the Amazon skeleton but puts people in front: a maker-of-the-week hero, the neighbourhood on every card, and rails phrased the way neighbours talk (“Pros your neighbours booked this month”). Red Hat Text sets the UI and its Display cut sets headings: one superfamily, no serif. A warm flour-coloured ground holds white cards with 14 px corners and full-bleed 4:3 photos. Bow River teal is the only action colour; Saskatoon berry marks deals. Selections are a white thumb with a teal ring and a check, so they never read as buttons, and chips are the only pills. Density is a step below Plainsight (4 results per row), which suits Services and Food better than Shop. It needs good lifestyle photography, so it depends most on the merchant photo work in S-181.

Amazon patterns it uses

  • Search-led header with an icon department strip (Services, Shop, Food) under it
  • Hero spotlight with two side tiles, four category tiles, then white rail panels
  • Left filter rail and chips; 4-up cards with rating first, then price and delivery
  • Gallery, details and a soft-shadow buy box
  • One-page checkout as separate cards per step
  • Seller Central-style action cards and dense tables on a warm back office

Direction C

Chinook

Bold and modern. A deep violet header, big department tiles and sticker prices: confident, fast, and clearly its own brand.

Chinook is the direction with the most brand. A deep violet header (deliberately far from navy) opens into three large department tiles, Book a pro, Shop local and Order in, so the three departments are obvious before any rail. Archivo is the only family: normal width for UI, stretched and heavy for headlines. Cards are tall 4:5 photos with soft shadows and 18 px corners; deals are yellow stickers. Violet is the only action colour, yellow is only ever a deal and never a button, and selected states are solid ink. It photographs food and people best and has the strongest presence on a phone. It is also the furthest from the current build and the most sensitive to bad photos, because the photos are large.

Amazon patterns it uses

  • Search-led header on a violet bar with a department strip under it
  • Three department tiles, then a deals rail, category tiles and order-again rails
  • Left filter rail and chips; 4-up tall cards with sticker deals
  • Gallery, details and a floating buy box with a sticky phone bar
  • One-page checkout in shadowed cards with violet step numbers
  • Seller Central-style action cards; dark sidebar selection; dense tables with a black bulk bar
